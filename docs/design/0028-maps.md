# 0028. Maps drawn from the caller's GeoJSON

Status: accepted, 2026-11-01. Builds on [0021](0021-native-points-and-reader-controls.md),
[0023](0023-the-figure-in-words.md) and [0027](0027-contrast-export-and-roles.md).

## Problem

Area 10's checklist item A15 asks for maps. germomics showed John Snow's 1854 Soho cholera map
through the Google Maps JavaScript API: a third-party script and key, tiles the reader's browser
fetches from Google, no data table and no keyboard walk, and colors a forced-colors reader cannot
see. Dustin decided on 2026-10-31 that Enarratio draws maps. The maps a network site needs are
small: cases by address over a few streets, rates by district.

## Decision

1. **A new entry, `enarratio/map`, with no dependencies.** `dotMap` and `choroplethMap` draw on
   the server, like every other chart, and return a figure with the title, finding, summary,
   data table and CSV.
2. **Enarratio ships no boundaries.** Callers pass GeoJSON (RFC 7946: longitude and latitude in
   WGS 84) for the regions and for any basemap layer. Boundary files are large, carry their own
   licenses and go out of date; the caller knows which ones it needs and cites them. Every
   position is checked as it is read, and a bad one throws, naming the layer and feature.
3. **Projection.** By default equirectangular, longitude scaled by the cosine of the map's middle
   latitude: at city scale it is true to a fraction of a percent, needs no library and keeps
   north up. `"mercator"` is there to match a tiled map or for a country, and any function from
   longitude and latitude to planar x and y can be passed. The extent is fitted to the drawing
   with one scale for both axes, so a map is never stretched; its height follows its shape unless
   given.
4. **Dot map.** A point's value is its dot's area (radius by the square root), the convention
   for proportional symbols, with a key of three reference sizes. Big dots are drawn first, so a
   small one is never hidden under a big one; a zero is a small ring. Landmarks (pumps,
   stations) are squares, apart from the dots; one may be highlighted, filled, larger and named
   on the map. The largest dot is 9 units across at the default width and less on a narrow
   drawing (never under 5), so dots do not merge on a phone.
5. **Choropleth.** Regions take the theme's five sequential steps, with the steps labeled in the
   ramp legend, as the heatmap does. A value is never shown by color alone: each region prints
   its value where it fits, in the step's text color; a region with no data (a `null`, or a
   region drawn but absent from the data) is outlined dashed, not shaded as zero, and is listed
   in the table as "no data". Neighbors in one step are parted by a line in the background color.
6. **Basemap.** Streets, rivers and areas sit under the data in the muted text color at reduced
   strength and the grid color, clipped to the map. Names are placed along the longest stretch
   of each named line inside the map (or at an area's centroid), beside the line rather than on
   it, never upside down, trying the middle and then places either side until one is clear of
   the dots, the landmarks and the other names; a name with no clear place is left out. Lines
   are clipped to the map (Liang-Barsky), so a street that runs past the edge still gets its
   name.
7. **Reading order.** With `enarratio/enhance`, the dots or regions are walked by keyboard as a
   page is read: in eight bands from north to south, west to east within each. Each point's name
   gives its label and value, and its place in the walk.

## Not done here

- No tiles, zoom or pan: a map that needs them is not a figure. A page that wants a slippy map
  can link the data table's coordinates to one.
- No bundled projections beyond the two; a caller who needs Albers or a polar projection passes
  a function (for example from d3-geo).
- germomics moves its Soho map onto `dotMap` in its own pull request, with Snow's streets from
  lindbrook's cholera data, cited there.
